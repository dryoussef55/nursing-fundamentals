Vital signs

Normal adult ranges

SignNormal range (adult)
Temperature36.5 to 37.5 °C (oral average about 37.0 °C)
Pulse60 to 100 beats/min
Respirations12 to 20 breaths/min
Blood pressurearound 120/80 mmHg; below 90/60 is generally low (hypotension)
Oxygen saturation (SpO2)95% or higher on room air

Key points

  • Compare every reading with the patient's baseline, not only the textbook range.
  • Report abnormal values to the nurse in charge or physician; know your faculty's exact reporting limits.
  • Count respirations without telling the patient, so they do not change their breathing.
Hand hygiene

WHO Five Moments

  • 1. Before touching the patient
  • 2. Before a clean or aseptic procedure
  • 3. After body fluid exposure risk
  • 4. After touching the patient
  • 5. After touching the patient's surroundings

Technique

  • Soap and water: 40 to 60 seconds, covering all surfaces of hands and wrists.
  • Alcohol-based hand rub: 20 to 30 seconds, until dry.
  • Wash with soap and water when hands are visibly soiled, and after caring for patients with suspected spore-forming infections such as Clostridioides difficile.
Patient safety

Identification and falls

  • Use two identifiers (for example full name and date of birth, or name plus ID number) before any medication, procedure or sample collection. Never use the room or bed number alone.
  • Keep the call bell within the patient's reach at all times.
  • Keep the bed in its lowest position when leaving the patient; use side rails according to hospital policy.
  • Check the floor for spills, clutter and loose cables, especially for elderly or dizzy patients.
Infection control

Standard precautions

  • Treat blood and all body fluids (except sweat) as potentially infectious, for every patient, whether or not an infection is known.
  • Hand hygiene before and after every patient contact, and after removing gloves.
  • Gloves when contact with blood, body fluids, mucous membranes or broken skin is possible. Change gloves between tasks on the same patient.
  • Mask, eye protection and gown when splashes are likely.
  • Never recap used needles by hand; dispose of sharps immediately in a sharps container.
Nursing process

ADPIE

  • Assessment: collect subjective and objective data.
  • Diagnosis: analyze data and identify the patient's problems (nursing diagnoses).
  • Planning: set goals and choose interventions, with the patient.
  • Implementation: carry out the planned care.
  • Evaluation: check whether goals were met, then reassess and adjust.
Positioning

Common positions

  • Fowler's: head of bed 45 to 60 degrees. Helps breathing and eating.
  • Semi-Fowler's: head of bed 30 to 45 degrees.
  • Supine: flat on the back.
  • Prone: lying on the abdomen.
  • Lateral (side-lying): on the side, often with pillows for alignment.
  • Sims': semi-prone, on the left side with the right knee drawn up; often used for rectal procedures.

Key point

  • Reposition immobile patients regularly (commonly every 2 hours) to prevent pressure injuries; follow your clinical site's schedule.
